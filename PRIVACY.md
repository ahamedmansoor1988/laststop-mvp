# QuikLook Privacy Policy

**Last updated: 22 August 2026**

QuikLook reminds you to check your belongings before you get out of a vehicle. This policy
explains exactly what the app does with your information.

The short version: **QuikLook has no servers.** There is no QuikLook account, no QuikLook
database, and nowhere for us to store anything about you. Everything the app knows about you is
kept on your own phone. Two features send data to Google in order to work, and those are
described precisely below.

## What stays on your phone, and only on your phone

All of the following is stored in the app's private storage on your device. It is never
transmitted to us, because there is no "us" to transmit it to:

- Your home address and any other saved places
- Recently used destinations
- Your belongings checklist
- Trip settings — timer length, destination, travel mode, and whether a trip is running
- Profile details you enter during setup: name, age, gender, and phone number
- Your Google account's name, email address, and profile photo URL, if you choose to sign in

Uninstalling QuikLook deletes all of it.

## What is sent to Google, and why

QuikLook uses two Google services. In both cases the data goes directly from your phone to
Google — it does not pass through any server of ours.

**Google Routes API — only during a destination trip.** When you start a trip with a
destination, the app sends your current latitude and longitude together with your destination's
latitude and longitude to Google, so it can calculate how long you have left. It asks Google for
nothing but the travel duration and distance. This happens repeatedly while that trip is
running, at a frequency that adapts to how far away you are. It does **not** happen during
timer-based trips, and it does **not** happen when no trip is running.

**Google Places — only while you search.** When you type into an address search field, what you
type is sent to Google to return suggestions.

Google's handling of this data is governed by the
[Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

**Google Sign-In** is optional. If you use it, Google confirms your identity and returns your
name, email, and photo URL to the app, which stores them on your device. You can skip sign-in
entirely and use the whole app without it.

## Location, specifically

QuikLook requests location access **while the app is in use**. It does not request the "all the
time" background location permission.

Location is used to notice that you have started travelling, and to work out when you are
approaching the end of a trip so it can alert you in time. Location readings are held on your
device and are sent off it only in the one case described above: Google Routes, during a
destination trip.

QuikLook runs a foreground service while watching for travel or tracking a trip. Android
requires a permanent notification for this, which is why you will see one — it is also your
signal that the app is active, and you can stop tracking from it at any time.

## What QuikLook does not do

- No advertising, and no ad networks or advertising identifiers
- No analytics or usage tracking
- No selling or sharing of personal information
- No profile building, and no location history beyond what a running trip needs
- No account, and no data held by us after uninstall

## Children

QuikLook is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from
them.

## Your choices

- **Location** — revocable at any time in Android Settings › Apps › QuikLook › Permissions. The
  travel-detection and arrival features stop working without it; the rest of the app still runs.
- **Sign-in** — skippable, and you can sign out from the app's Settings screen.
- **Profile details** — all optional; the setup step can be skipped.
- **Everything else** — uninstalling removes all locally stored data.

## Changes

will be noted in the app's release notes.
